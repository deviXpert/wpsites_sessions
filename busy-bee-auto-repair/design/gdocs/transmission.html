How Does Your Vehicle's Transmission Work?

The transmission transfers engine power to the drive wheels while adjusting the gear ratio to suit your speed and driving conditions.

Most passenger vehicles use one of several transmission designs:

  • Automatic transmission: Changes gears automatically as vehicle speed and engine load change.
  • Continuously Variable Transmission (CVT): Uses a variable gear ratio rather than a traditional set of fixed gears.
  • Dual-Clutch Transmission (DCT): Uses two clutches to manage gear changes, often providing quick shifts.
  • Manual transmission: Requires the driver to select gears using a gear lever and clutch pedal.

Each type has different components, fluid requirements and maintenance needs. A repair procedure that is suitable for one transmission may not be appropriate for another.

That is why identifying your vehicle's transmission type and following the manufacturer's service information are important parts of the repair process.

Transmission Diagnosis and Road Testing

A transmission problem should not be diagnosed by guessing from one symptom. Slipping, harsh shifts and delayed engagement can have several possible causes.

Our diagnostic process may include scanning for relevant fault codes, checking transmission fluid where applicable and road testing the vehicle to understand how it behaves under normal driving conditions.

We look at the symptoms and available diagnostic information together before recommending the next step.

Transmission Fluid Change and Filter Service

Transmission fluid helps lubricate moving components, manage heat and support the hydraulic operation of many transmission systems. Over time, fluid can deteriorate or become contaminated.

A fluid and filter service may be recommended when it is due according to the manufacturer's maintenance schedule or when inspection indicates that service is appropriate.

The correct fluid specification matters. Using an unsuitable fluid can affect shifting and transmission operation, so the vehicle's requirements should always be checked before service.

Transmission Flush Service

A transmission flush is different from a standard drain-and-fill service. The appropriate procedure depends on the transmission design, fluid condition and manufacturer recommendations.

A flush is not automatically the right solution for every transmission problem. If a vehicle is already slipping, overheating or showing signs of internal damage, the cause should be investigated before deciding on any fluid service.

Our team can assess the situation and explain whether a fluid change, filter service, flush or further diagnosis is more suitable.

Transmission Leak Inspection and Repair

Transmission fluid leaks can develop around seals, gaskets, cooler lines, connections or other parts of the system.

You may notice fluid beneath the vehicle or a change in shifting performance. Some transmission fluids are red when new, but colour can vary depending on the fluid type and condition.

A leak should not be ignored. Low fluid levels can affect lubrication, hydraulic pressure and temperature control. Identifying where the fluid is escaping is the first step toward the correct repair.

Solenoid and Sensor Repair

Modern automatic transmissions use electronic controls to manage gear selection and shifting behaviour. Solenoids help control fluid flow, while sensors provide information about speed, temperature and operating conditions.

A faulty solenoid, damaged connection or incorrect sensor signal may cause harsh shifting, delayed gear changes or transmission-related warning codes.

A fault code can point toward a particular circuit or system, but it does not always confirm that a specific part has failed. Additional testing may be needed before replacing components.

Transmission Cooler Line Inspection and Repair

Many automatic transmissions use a cooler to help manage operating temperature. Fluid travels through cooler lines between the transmission and cooling system.

A damaged line or leaking connection can allow fluid to escape. If the fluid level drops too far, the transmission may not operate correctly.

An inspection can help identify damaged lines, loose connections and other possible sources of leakage.

Manual Transmission Clutch Repair

In a manual vehicle, the clutch connects and disconnects the engine from the transmission so you can change gears.

A worn clutch may cause engine RPM to rise without a matching increase in vehicle speed. Other symptoms can include difficulty selecting gears, clutch slipping, unusual noises or changes in pedal feel.

The clutch system should be inspected to determine whether the issue involves the clutch disc, pressure plate, release components or another related part.

Common Signs Your Transmission Needs Attention

Transmission problems do not always begin with a major failure. Small changes in shifting behaviour can be early indications that something needs checking.

Watch for these warning signs:

1. Gears slipping

The engine RPM may rise while your vehicle struggles to accelerate. The transmission may also seem to change gears unexpectedly.

2. Delayed gear engagement

You may notice a pause after selecting Drive or Reverse before the vehicle begins moving. This can be associated with fluid, hydraulic or mechanical issues.

3. Harsh or jerky shifting

A noticeable jolt when changing gears may indicate a problem with transmission operation, control systems or other related components.

4. Unusual whining or humming noises

Some transmission faults can produce whining, humming or grinding sounds. The source should be checked because similar noises can also come from other vehicle components.

5. Transmission fluid leaking

Fluid underneath your parked vehicle may indicate a leak. If you suspect transmission fluid loss, have the vehicle inspected rather than continuing to drive without checking the fluid level.

6. Burning smell

An unusual burning smell can be a sign of overheating or fluid problems. If the smell is accompanied by abnormal shifting or a temperature warning, stop somewhere safe and arrange an inspection.

7. Warning lights or fault messages

A check engine light or transmission warning may indicate an issue with the transmission control system or another related component. A diagnostic scan can help identify where further testing is needed.

What Causes Transmission Problems?

Several factors can affect transmission performance. Understanding the possible causes helps explain why an inspection is important before choosing a repair.

Old or Incorrect Transmission Fluid

Transmission fluid can lose its effectiveness over time. Using the wrong fluid specification may also affect lubrication, pressure and shifting performance.

Excessive Heat

Heat is one of the major challenges for transmission systems. Heavy loads, demanding driving conditions, cooling-system problems or internal faults can contribute to excessive operating temperatures.

Electrical and Sensor Faults

Modern transmissions depend on electronic signals to control shifting. A damaged wire, poor connection, faulty sensor or solenoid problem may affect how the transmission responds.

Component Wear

Clutches, friction materials, seals, bearings and other internal components can wear with use. The symptoms and required repair depend on which parts are affected.

Neglected Maintenance

Skipping manufacturer-recommended service can allow fluid condition and other maintenance concerns to go unchecked. Following the correct service schedule can help reduce avoidable wear.

Transmission Service: How Often Should You Change the Fluid?

There is no single fluid-change interval that applies to every vehicle.

Some manufacturers recommend transmission fluid service at specific mileage or time intervals. Others have different requirements depending on the transmission design and driving conditions.

The interval may also be affected by towing, frequent stop-and-go traffic, high temperatures and other demanding use.

Rather than relying on a general mileage figure, check your owner's manual or the manufacturer's maintenance schedule. If you notice slipping, delayed engagement or unusual noises, arrange an inspection instead of assuming a fluid change will solve the problem.

Does Every Transmission Problem Require a Rebuild?

No. A complete rebuild is not the automatic answer to every transmission complaint.

Depending on the cause, a repair may involve:

  • Correcting a fluid leak
  • Replacing or servicing a filter
  • Performing an appropriate fluid service
  • Repairing a solenoid or sensor
  • Addressing a cooler line or connection
  • Investigating a clutch problem in a manual transmission

However, significant internal wear or damage may require more extensive work. The condition of the transmission and the results of testing determine which options are realistic.

Frequently Asked Questions About Transmission Repair

1. How do I know if my transmission is failing?

Common signs include slipping gears, delayed engagement, harsh shifting, unusual noises, fluid leaks and burning smells. These symptoms do not always mean the transmission has failed completely, but they should be checked.

2. How much does transmission repair cost in Calgary?

The price depends on the transmission type, the cause of the problem, parts required and labour involved. A fluid service or sensor repair will have different costs from extensive internal transmission work. An inspection is needed to provide an accurate estimate.

3. Can I drive with a slipping transmission?

It is not recommended to continue driving with a slipping transmission. Slipping can indicate a problem that may worsen with continued use. If the vehicle loses drive, shifts unpredictably or shows signs of overheating, stop somewhere safe and arrange professional assistance.

4. How often should transmission fluid be changed?

The correct interval varies by vehicle and transmission type. Check the owner's manual or manufacturer service schedule rather than following one mileage recommendation for every car.

5. What colour should transmission fluid be?

New automatic transmission fluid is often red, but colour depends on the fluid specification. Fluid can darken with age and use. Colour alone cannot confirm whether the transmission is healthy, so condition and manufacturer requirements should also be considered.

6. What causes a delay when shifting into Drive or Reverse?

Delayed engagement may be related to fluid level or condition, hydraulic pressure, solenoids or internal wear. A proper inspection is needed to identify the cause.

7. Is a transmission flush always necessary?

No. A flush is not required for every vehicle or every transmission problem. The appropriate service depends on the manufacturer's recommendations, transmission design and fluid condition.

8. Can a faulty sensor cause transmission problems?

Yes. Transmission control systems use sensor information to manage gear changes. A faulty sensor or wiring issue can affect shifting, but diagnostic testing is needed to confirm the source.

9. What does it mean when my transmission makes a whining noise?

A whining noise may be related to fluid, pressure, bearings or other internal components. Similar sounds can also come from other parts of the vehicle, so the source should be identified before repairs are recommended.

10. What is the difference between transmission repair and transmission rebuilding?

Transmission repair usually refers to fixing a specific problem or replacing affected components. A rebuild involves more extensive disassembly, inspection and replacement of worn or damaged internal parts. The right approach depends on the transmission's condition.

11. Does a manual transmission need fluid service?

Yes. Manual transmissions use lubricant to protect internal gears and components. The type of fluid and service interval depend on the vehicle manufacturer's specifications.

12. Can low transmission fluid cause slipping?

Yes. In many automatic transmissions, low fluid can affect hydraulic pressure and lubrication, leading to poor shifting or slipping. If you suspect a leak or low fluid level, have it checked using the correct procedure for your vehicle.
