New Tires in Calgary for Different Driving Needs

Not every driver needs the same type of tire. Your choice should depend on the vehicle, typical road conditions, driving habits and the season.

We offer different tire options, including all-season, winter and all-weather tires, subject to availability.

All-Season Tires

All-season tires are designed for a range of road and temperature conditions. They can be a practical choice for drivers who mainly travel on cleared roads and experience moderate seasonal changes.

However, all-season tires are not the same as dedicated winter tires. Their rubber compounds and tread designs may not provide the same cold-weather grip as tires specifically designed for winter driving.

Winter Tires

Calgary winters can bring snow, ice and sudden temperature drops. Winter tires are made with rubber compounds that remain more flexible in cold conditions, helping maintain traction when temperatures fall.

Their tread patterns are also designed to improve grip on snow-covered and slippery roads.

Consider winter tires if you regularly drive during colder months, commute early in the morning or travel on roads that are not always cleared.

All-Weather Tires

All-weather tires are designed for year-round use and carry a winter certification marking when approved for severe snow conditions. They offer an alternative for drivers who prefer not to change between separate summer and winter sets.

They are different from standard all-season tires, so check the tyre markings and manufacturer specifications before choosing.

Our team can help you compare available options based on your vehicle's tyre size and intended use.

Correct installation is an important part of replacing your tyres. A tyre that is not fitted or balanced properly can affect vehicle handling and may cause vibration.

A typical tyre replacement involves:

  1. Checking the tyre size and confirming compatibility with the vehicle.
  2. Removing the existing wheel and tyre.
  3. Inspecting the wheel and relevant components for visible concerns.

When Can a Flat Tire Be Repaired?

A puncture may be repairable when it is located in the appropriate area of the tread and meets recognised repair criteria.

A repair may not be suitable when:

  • The puncture is in the sidewall or shoulder area.
  • The tyre has been driven while severely underinflated.
  • There is significant internal damage.
  • The tyre has a bulge, exposed cords or serious structural deterioration.
  • The damage is too large or there are multiple unsuitable repairs.

A temporary plug from a roadside kit is not necessarily a permanent repair. A proper assessment is needed to determine whether the tyre can safely remain in service.

If the tyre cannot be repaired, replacement may be the more appropriate option. We can help you understand the available choices.

Tire Rotation – Helping Your Tires Wear More Evenly

Tyres do not always wear at the same rate. The front tyres may experience different loads and steering forces from the rear tyres, depending on the vehicle and its drivetrain.

Regular tire rotation changes the position of the tyres to help distribute wear more evenly.

The correct rotation pattern depends on factors such as:

  • Whether the vehicle is front-wheel, rear-wheel or all-wheel drive.
  • Whether the tyres are directional.
  • Whether front and rear tyre sizes are different.
  • The tyre manufacturer's rotation recommendations.

Many vehicles benefit from rotation at intervals specified in the owner's manual. Some drivers combine tyre rotation with routine maintenance visits to make it easier to keep track.

Even wear can help you get more consistent performance from your set of tyres. However, rotation cannot correct alignment problems, worn suspension parts or tyres that are already damaged.

Wheel Balancing – What Causes Steering Wheel Vibration?

If your steering wheel or vehicle starts vibrating at certain speeds, unbalanced wheels may be one possible cause.

Wheel balancing distributes weight around the wheel and tyre assembly. Even a small imbalance can become noticeable as the vehicle gains speed.

Common signs that balancing may be needed include:

  • Steering wheel vibration at highway speeds.
  • Vibration felt through the seat or vehicle floor.
  • Uneven tyre wear associated with other wheel or suspension issues.
  • Vibration that starts after installing replacement tyres.

Balancing is not the same as wheel alignment. Balancing corrects uneven weight distribution in the wheel assembly, while alignment adjusts wheel angles according to vehicle specifications.

If your car pulls to one side or the steering wheel sits off-centre, you may need a wheel alignment service in Calgary rather than balancing alone.

When Should You Replace Your Tires?

Tyre replacement should be based on condition, not just appearance. A tyre can look acceptable from the outside but have damage or wear that affects its performance.

Pay attention to these warning signs:

Low tread depth: Shallow tread reduces the tyre's ability to channel water and maintain grip. Our current service page identifies tread at or below 4/32 inch, approximately 3 mm, as a concern for Calgary snow driving.

Sidewall bulges or cuts: A bulge can indicate internal structural damage. Do not ignore it or assume that adding air will solve the problem.

Cracks in the rubber: Visible cracking can be a sign of ageing or deterioration.

Repeated air loss: A tyre that keeps losing pressure may have a puncture, damaged valve or another leak.

Uneven tread wear: One edge wearing faster than the other can point to alignment, inflation or suspension issues.

Older tyres: Rubber can deteriorate with age. The tyre's manufacturing date and condition should both be considered, even if the tread is not fully worn.

If you notice any of these signs, arrange an inspection before relying on the tyres for regular driving.

Preparing Your Tires for Calgary's Changing Seasons

Calgary drivers need to think about more than tread depth. Temperature changes, snowfall and road conditions can all influence tyre performance.

Here are a few practical ways to prepare:

  • Check tyre pressure regularly, particularly when temperatures change.
  • Inspect tread condition before the winter season.
  • Consider winter-rated tyres for regular cold-weather driving.
  • Check for cracks, bulges and embedded objects.
  • Arrange rotation according to your vehicle's maintenance schedule.
  • Make sure your spare tyre, if equipped, is in usable condition.

A common guideline is to consider switching to winter tires when temperatures consistently fall below approximately 7°C. The exact timing depends on weather conditions and your driving needs, so avoid waiting until the first major snowfall.

For example, if the inside edge of a tyre wears much faster than the outside, incorrect wheel alignment could be one possible cause. If the vehicle bounces excessively or produces clunking sounds over bumps, worn suspension components may need attention.

Sometimes. A small puncture in the repairable tread area may be suitable, depending on its size and the tyre's condition. Sidewall damage and certain types of internal damage generally require replacement.

7. What is the difference between wheel balancing and wheel alignment?

Balancing corrects uneven weight distribution in a wheel and tyre assembly. Alignment adjusts wheel angles to the vehicle manufacturer's specifications. Both can affect driving comfort and tyre wear, but they solve different problems.

8. How often should I rotate my tires?

Follow your vehicle and tyre manufacturer's recommendations. Many vehicles have rotation intervals listed in the owner's manual. The correct pattern depends on the drivetrain and tyre design.

9. Are all-weather tires the same as all-season tires?

No. All-weather tyres are designed for year-round use and carry a winter certification marking when approved for severe snow conditions. Standard all-season tyres do not necessarily provide the same winter capability.

10. Can I replace only one tire instead of all four?

It depends on the vehicle, drivetrain, remaining tread depth and tyre specifications. Some all-wheel-drive vehicles have strict requirements for differences in tyre circumference. Check the manufacturer's guidance before replacing only one tyre.

11. Why does my steering wheel shake at highway speeds?

Unbalanced wheels are one possible cause, but tyre damage, uneven wear, wheel problems or suspension components can also create vibration. An inspection can help identify the source.
