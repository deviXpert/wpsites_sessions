How Does Your Car's Cooling System Work?

The cooling system moves heat away from the engine and helps maintain a suitable operating temperature. Several components work together to make this happen.

  • Radiator: Transfers heat from the hot coolant to the surrounding air.
  • Water pump: Circulates coolant through the engine and radiator.
  • Thermostat: Regulates coolant flow based on engine temperature.
  • Coolant hoses: Carry coolant between the engine, radiator and other parts of the system.
  • Cooling fan: Helps move air through the radiator, especially when the vehicle is moving slowly or sitting in traffic.
  • Coolant: Absorbs and transfers heat while providing protection against freezing and corrosion.

A problem with one component can affect the performance of the entire system. For example, a working radiator cannot maintain the correct temperature if the water pump is not circulating coolant properly.

That is why cooling system diagnosis should consider the connected components rather than focusing only on the radiator.

Radiator Repair and Replacement

The radiator is responsible for releasing heat from the coolant as it passes through its internal channels. Over time, corrosion, physical damage, leaks or internal blockages can affect its performance.

Common radiator problems include:

  • Coolant leaking from the radiator
  • Visible corrosion or damaged sections
  • Cracks around the radiator tank
  • Restricted coolant flow
  • Repeated engine overheating

Depending on the condition and design of the radiator, repair or replacement may be recommended. If the radiator cannot maintain proper coolant flow or has significant damage, replacing it may be the more suitable option.

Coolant Flush and Refill

Coolant does more than prevent the engine from overheating. It also helps protect cooling system components against corrosion and freezing.

As coolant ages, its protective properties can deteriorate. Contamination or the use of an incorrect coolant type can also create problems.

A coolant service may involve draining old fluid, following the appropriate flushing procedure when required, and refilling the system with the correct coolant mixture.

The correct fluid specification matters. Different vehicles use different coolant formulations, so mixing incompatible products should be avoided.

Thermostat Replacement

The thermostat controls when coolant flows through the radiator. When the engine is cold, it helps the engine reach its normal operating temperature. Once the required temperature is reached, it opens to allow coolant to circulate through the radiator.

A thermostat that becomes stuck closed may restrict coolant flow and cause overheating. If it remains open, the engine may take longer to warm up or operate below its intended temperature.

A faulty thermostat can also contribute to inconsistent cabin heating. Testing helps determine whether the thermostat is responsible or whether another cooling system component needs attention.

Water Pump Replacement

The water pump keeps coolant moving through the engine and cooling system. Without proper circulation, heat cannot be transferred away from the engine effectively.

Signs of a water pump problem may include:

  • Coolant leaking near the pump
  • Unusual grinding or whining sounds
  • Engine temperature rising
  • Visible damage or corrosion
  • Coolant circulation problems

Some water pumps are driven by the engine's timing belt or another belt system, while others use different designs. The replacement procedure depends on the vehicle.

Coolant Hose and Clamp Replacement

Coolant hoses connect different sections of the cooling system. Heat, pressure and age can cause them to weaken, crack or become swollen.

A damaged hose or loose clamp may allow coolant to escape, particularly when the engine is hot and the system is under pressure.

During an inspection, hoses and connections can be checked for visible leaks, cracks, swelling and signs of deterioration. Replacing a damaged hose or clamp can help restore a secure connection.

Cooling System Pressure Testing and Leak Detection

Not every coolant leak is easy to see. Some leaks occur only when the engine reaches operating temperature or when the cooling system is under pressure.

A pressure test can help identify leaks that may not be obvious during a basic visual inspection.

The inspection may involve checking the radiator, hoses, connections, water pump and other accessible cooling system components. Finding the source is important because repeatedly topping up coolant will not fix an active leak.

Heater Core Diagnosis

Your vehicle's heater uses heat from the engine coolant to warm the cabin. The heater core acts as a small heat exchanger, allowing warm coolant to transfer heat to the air entering the passenger compartment.

If the heater stops producing warm air, the issue may involve low coolant, trapped air, restricted coolant flow, a thermostat problem or a faulty heater core.

A heater core leak may also cause a sweet smell inside the cabin, fogging on the windows or damp carpet. These symptoms should be inspected rather than treated as a normal heating problem.

Warning Signs Your Cooling System Needs Repair

Cooling system problems can develop gradually or appear suddenly. Pay attention to changes in temperature, coolant level and the way your vehicle operates.

1. Engine temperature rising

If the temperature gauge moves higher than normal, the cooling system may not be removing enough heat. A gauge entering the red zone is a serious warning.

2. Coolant leaking underneath the vehicle

Puddles or drips may indicate a leak from a radiator, hose, water pump or connection. Coolant can come in different colours depending on its formulation.

3. Sweet smell around the vehicle

A sweet smell may indicate leaking coolant. You might notice it after parking or when the engine is running.

4. Steam coming from under the hood

Steam can be a sign of overheating or coolant escaping onto hot engine components. Stop driving safely and switch off the engine.

5. Heater not producing warm air

A heater that suddenly blows cold air can sometimes indicate low coolant or poor circulation. It may also have a separate heating system fault.

6. Coolant level dropping repeatedly

If you need to add coolant regularly, there may be a leak or another problem that needs investigation. A healthy cooling system should not require frequent unexplained top-ups.

7. Temperature changes during traffic

If the temperature rises while sitting in traffic but improves when driving, possible causes include a cooling fan problem, restricted airflow or another cooling system fault.

These symptoms can overlap. A proper inspection is needed to identify the cause rather than replacing parts based on one sign.

What Should You Do If Your Car Overheats?

Driving with an overheating engine can lead to serious damage. Excessive heat may affect engine seals, cylinder head components and other internal parts.

If your temperature gauge rises into the red or you see steam:

  1. Find a safe place to pull over as soon as possible.
  2. Switch off the engine once you have stopped safely.
  3. Do not open the radiator cap or coolant reservoir cap while the system is hot.
  4. Keep away from escaping steam and hot coolant.
  5. Allow the vehicle to cool and arrange professional assistance.

Never pour cold water or coolant into a hot engine. Sudden temperature changes can create additional risks, and opening a pressurised cooling system can cause serious burns.

If the vehicle has overheated severely, it is safer to arrange towing rather than continuing to drive.

Why Do Cars Overheat in Calgary?

Calgary drivers experience a wide range of weather conditions throughout the year. Both summer heat and winter cold can place additional demands on vehicle cooling systems.

During warmer months, the cooling system must manage engine heat while outside temperatures are higher. Traffic congestion and extended idling can also make cooling fan performance important.

In winter, the coolant must provide suitable freeze protection. Incorrect coolant concentration or an unsuitable fluid can reduce the system's ability to protect the engine in cold weather.

Other factors can contribute to overheating at any time of year, including:

  • Low coolant caused by a leak
  • A damaged radiator
  • A thermostat that does not operate correctly
  • A failing water pump
  • A cooling fan that is not working properly
  • Restricted airflow or coolant circulation

Seasonal checks and following the manufacturer's maintenance schedule can help identify cooling system concerns before they become urgent.

How Often Should Coolant Be Changed?

Coolant service intervals vary by vehicle, coolant formulation and manufacturer recommendations. Some vehicles have longer service intervals than others.

The general recommendation should always come from your owner's manual or the manufacturer's maintenance schedule. A blanket interval does not suit every vehicle.

If the coolant looks contaminated, the level keeps dropping or the engine has been overheating, arrange an inspection instead of waiting for the next scheduled service.

Using the correct coolant specification is just as important as changing it at the right time.

Frequently Asked Questions About Radiator Repair

1. How much does radiator repair cost in Calgary?

The cost depends on the vehicle, the source of the problem and whether the repair involves a hose, radiator, thermostat, water pump or another component. An inspection is needed before an accurate estimate can be provided.

2. What are the common signs of a bad radiator?

Common signs include coolant leaks, repeated overheating, visible corrosion, damaged radiator sections and unexplained coolant loss. A restricted radiator may also affect cooling performance.

3. Can I drive my car if it is overheating?

No. Continuing to drive an overheating vehicle can cause serious engine damage. Pull over safely, switch off the engine and arrange professional assistance. Do not open the radiator cap while the system is hot.

4. How often should radiator coolant be changed?

The interval depends on the vehicle and coolant type. Follow the manufacturer's recommended service schedule rather than using one interval for every car.

5. Why does my car keep losing coolant?

Possible causes include leaking hoses, a damaged radiator, a faulty water pump, loose connections or other cooling system problems. If the level keeps dropping, the source should be identified.

6. Can a faulty thermostat cause overheating?

Yes. A thermostat stuck in the closed position can restrict coolant flow through the radiator and cause the engine to overheat.

7. What does a water pump do in a car?

The water pump circulates coolant through the engine and cooling system. If it fails or develops a leak, coolant circulation may be affected and engine temperature can rise.

8. Why is my car heater blowing cold air?

Possible causes include low coolant, trapped air, restricted coolant flow, a faulty thermostat or a heater core problem. The cooling and heating systems may need to be inspected together.

9. What causes coolant to leak underneath my car?

Coolant may escape from a damaged radiator, cracked hose, loose clamp, water pump or another connection. The location of the puddle can provide clues, but it does not always identify the exact source.

10. Is a coolant flush the same as a coolant change?

They are related but not always the same procedure. A coolant change generally involves draining and refilling the system, while a flush may involve additional steps to remove deposits or contamination. The correct method depends on the vehicle and condition of the coolant.

11. Can a radiator problem damage the engine?

Yes. If the radiator cannot remove enough heat, the engine may overheat. Severe overheating can damage internal engine components and lead to expensive repairs.

12. How do I know if my radiator or water pump is faulty?

Both can cause overheating and coolant loss. A radiator issue may involve leaks or restricted heat transfer, while a water pump problem may affect coolant circulation or cause leakage near the pump. Inspection and appropriate testing help distinguish between them.
