What Is Wheel Alignment and Why Does It Matter?

Wheel alignment refers to adjusting the angles of your vehicle's wheels so they sit in the correct position according to the manufacturer's specifications.

These angles influence how your tyres contact the road and how your vehicle responds when you steer. Even a small change in alignment can affect handling and cause tyres to wear unevenly.

Misalignment can develop gradually or happen after an impact. Driving over a deep pothole, hitting a curb or replacing certain suspension components can change the wheel angles.

A proper wheel alignment helps:

  • Keep your vehicle tracking straight on the road.
  • Improve steering response and driving stability.
  • Reduce uneven tyre wear.
  • Maintain the intended handling characteristics of your vehicle.
  • Help protect your investment in new tyres.

Alignment does not repair damaged tyres or worn suspension components. If another mechanical issue is responsible, it needs to be addressed before the alignment can hold correctly.

Three Main Wheel Alignment Angles Explained

Wheel alignment involves several measurements. Three important angles are camber, caster and toe. Understanding them helps explain why a vehicle may behave differently when its alignment is incorrect.

1. Camber – The Inward or Outward Wheel Angle

Camber describes how much the top of a wheel tilts inward or outward when viewed from the front of the vehicle.

  • Positive camber: The top of the wheel tilts outward.
  • Negative camber: The top of the wheel tilts inward.
  • Incorrect camber: Can contribute to uneven wear across the inside or outside edge of a tyre.

The correct camber setting depends on the vehicle's design and manufacturer specifications. It should not be adjusted based on appearance alone.

2. Caster – Steering Stability and Directional Control

Caster is the angle of the steering axis when viewed from the side of the vehicle. It plays an important role in steering stability and how the steering wheel returns towards its centre after a turn.

Incorrect caster can contribute to:

  • Steering that feels unstable.
  • Difficulty maintaining a straight direction.
  • Different steering behaviour between the left and right sides.

Caster specifications vary between vehicles, and not every vehicle allows direct caster adjustment.

3. Toe – The Direction Your Wheels Point

Toe describes whether the front edges of the tyres point slightly inward or outward when viewed from above.

  • Toe-in: The front edges point slightly towards each other.
  • Toe-out: The front edges point slightly away from each other.

Incorrect toe settings can cause rapid or uneven tyre wear, even when other alignment angles are within specification.

A technician measures these angles and compares the readings with the manufacturer's recommended settings.

Signs Your Car Needs a Wheel Alignment in Calgary

Alignment problems are not always obvious at first. However, your vehicle may show several warning signs while driving.

Your Vehicle Pulls to One Side

If your car consistently drifts left or right on a reasonably level road, alignment could be one possible cause. Tyre pressure differences, brake problems and road conditions can also affect how a vehicle tracks.

Steering Wheel Is Not Straight

When driving straight, your steering wheel should generally sit in its intended centred position. If it remains tilted to one side, the alignment or steering system may need inspection.

Uneven Tyre Wear

Check the inside and outside edges of your tyres. If one side is wearing faster than the other, incorrect alignment may be contributing to the problem.

Uneven wear can also result from incorrect tyre pressure, worn suspension parts or other mechanical issues.

Vehicle Feels Unstable

If your car feels like it is wandering or requires constant steering corrections, have the steering and suspension system checked. Alignment is one possible cause, but it should not be assumed without an inspection.

You Recently Hit a Pothole or Curb

Calgary roads can expose vehicles to potholes, rough surfaces and unexpected impacts. A hard hit can affect wheel alignment or damage suspension and steering components.

If you notice new handling problems after an impact, arranging an inspection is a sensible next step.

When Should You Get Your Wheels Aligned?

There is no single schedule that applies to every vehicle. Your driving conditions, vehicle design and maintenance history all matter.

Consider arranging a wheel alignment inspection in these situations:

After installing new tyres: Correct alignment helps protect your new tyres from premature uneven wear.

After suspension repairs: Replacing components such as control arms, tie rods or other suspension parts can change wheel angles. An alignment may be required after the repair.

Wheel Alignment vs Wheel Balancing – What Is the Difference?

Wheel alignment and wheel balancing are often confused because both services involve the wheels and tyres. However, they solve different problems.

Wheel alignment corrects the angles of the wheels so they meet the vehicle manufacturer's specifications. It is especially relevant when the vehicle pulls to one side, the steering wheel is off-centre or tyres wear unevenly.

Wheel balancing corrects uneven weight distribution in the wheel and tyre assembly. An imbalance can cause vibration through the steering wheel, seat or vehicle at certain speeds.

Sometimes a vehicle needs one service, while other situations may require both. The correct choice depends on the symptoms and inspection results.

Why Suspension Condition Matters Before Alignment

Wheel alignment depends on the condition of the components that hold and control your wheels.

For example, worn tie rod ends can introduce movement into the steering system. Damaged ball joints or control arm bushings can also affect how the wheels sit under load.

If these parts are worn, adjusting the alignment alone may not provide a lasting result.

That is why checking the steering and suspension system before making adjustments is important.

4. Is wheel alignment the same as wheel balancing?

No. Alignment adjusts wheel angles to manufacturer specifications, while balancing corrects uneven weight distribution in the wheel and tyre assembly. They address different problems.

5. Do I need an alignment after buying new tires?

It is sensible to check alignment when installing new tyres, especially if the old set showed uneven wear or the vehicle had steering problems. Correct alignment helps protect the replacement tyres.

Yes. A significant pothole impact can change alignment settings or damage steering and suspension components. If your vehicle starts pulling or the steering wheel changes position after an impact, arrange an inspection.

Yes. Incorrect camber or toe settings can contribute to uneven tyre wear. However, incorrect tyre pressure and worn suspension components can cause similar patterns.

8. Should I get an alignment after suspension repair?

Often, yes. Replacing steering or suspension components can change wheel angles. Whether alignment is required depends on the parts replaced and the vehicle's design.

9. Can wheel alignment fix a vibrating steering wheel?

Not necessarily. Vibration at certain speeds is often associated with wheel imbalance, tyre damage or other wheel and suspension problems. An inspection is needed to identify the cause.

10. How often should I check my wheel alignment?

There is no fixed interval for every vehicle. Check it when you notice steering changes, uneven tyre wear, after a significant road impact or when recommended following suspension work. An annual inspection can also be a useful maintenance reminder.

They are three important wheel alignment angles. Camber describes the inward or outward tilt of a wheel, caster relates to the steering axis angle, and toe describes whether the wheels point inward or outward when viewed from above.
